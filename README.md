# Scanplan

A browser-only 3D event-layout prototype inspired by the supplied warehouse photograph. It models exposed brick, concrete, steel roof trusses, high windows and skylights. The estimated 12 × 22 m footprint is illustrative, not measured or automatically reconstructed.

## DevDay Hack London — judge overview

**Plan the event. Walk the room before anyone arrives.** Scanplan helps event organisers explore a venue, arrange furniture, compare event layouts and preview the room with animated attendees before moving real tables.

Codex helped build and iterate on the 3D venue, editing controls, activity layouts, local GLB furniture library, crowd preview and cinematic tour, as well as automated geometry, model-validation and navigation tests. Three.js, OrbitControls and GLTFLoader are existing MIT-licensed dependencies; their license is included in `dist/vendor/`. The source photograph is not distributed, and venue dimensions are estimates.

For the submission description, demo walkthrough, attribution notes and latest-layout branch instructions, see [SUBMISSION.md](SUBMISSION.md). All Scanplan project code was built during the hackathon on 1 November 2026, as confirmed by Liem; existing third-party libraries are credited above. This prototype does not perform automatic room scanning or free-text AI planning.

## Run

Run `corepack pnpm dev` from this repository (Node.js 22.13+). No dependency installation is required.
Open http://localhost:8108. The server reads its port from `project.json`.
There are no runtime package dependencies. Three.js 0.170.0, OrbitControls and GLTFLoader are vendored under `dist/vendor/` with their MIT license so rendering needs no external CDN.

## Use

- Edit **Warehouse dimensions** (width, length, wall height and roof peak in metres), then **Apply dimensions**. The room rebuilds while furniture keeps its size. Furniture outside a smaller room moves inside; overlap warnings indicate where to rearrange it. Resizing is undoable. Width/length support 6–60 m, wall height 3–15 m and roof peak up to 20 m, at least 0.5 m above the walls.
- **People at your event:** enter a whole number from 0–500 (e.g. 100), choose **Sitting & walking**, **Walking around**, or **Sitting (standing if no seat)**, then **Add / update people**. Mixed mode aims to seat half the crowd, limited to available built-in chairs and couches (one per chair, three per couch); others walk. Seated mode leaves overflow standing. Pause/resume or remove the crowd without changing furniture. People update after layout edits and resizing, appear in every view including the venue film, and their settings persist and export with the layout. Undo/redo includes people settings; reset removes them. The status reports actual placement and any shortfall when floor space is full. Animated walkers avoid room boundaries and furniture footprints, including imported models; they may pass through each other. Uploaded models are treated as solid obstacles, not seats. This is an illustrative visual preview, not a crowd dynamics, capacity or evacuation simulation.
- Drag empty space to orbit; scroll or use +/− to zoom. Right-drag pans.
- Select **Floor plan** for overhead placement or **Inside** for an eye-level perspective.
- Select **Video** (2× playback; 14 seconds for the 28-second timeline) to enter a warehouse with 50 people already inside, walking around the current layout from the first frame. Fly down from outside, pass through opening double doors, walk through the room at 1.65 m eye height, and look around the gathering. The camera routes around furniture and guests keep the lens clear. The film uses its own crowd without changing saved people settings or furniture. Pause, replay and scrubbing reproduce the same movement. Packed rooms show only the people that can fit; a blocked entrance keeps the camera at the doorway. Reduced-motion preferences start the tour paused. This is a live animation, not an exported video file.
- Video guests keep a 1.4 m gap around the camera and pass at its sides. Side steps respect furniture and room boundaries; if an aisle is too narrow, the guest is kept out of the shot until clear. Pause, replay and scrubbing reproduce the same clearance.
- Drag furniture to move it; optionally snap to a 0.25 m grid. The inspector also accepts coordinates in metres, relative to the room centre.
- Add tables, couches and chairs from the catalogue; rotate, duplicate or delete selected items. R rotates, Delete removes and Escape deselects.
- **My library → Upload a 3D model:** choose a `.glb`, edit its name if needed, then **Add GLB to library**. Click the saved model to place it. Models retain their metre scale, are centred on their footprint and rest on the floor; their measured bounds drive selection, collisions and room limits. Uploaded furniture supports dragging, rotation, duplication, deletion from the room and undo/redo.
- The library stays in IndexedDB in this browser on this device and origin; it is not uploaded to a server or shared between browsers. Clearing site data removes it. Models persist independently of the room reset. Import limits: static GLB 2.0, embedded buffers/textures, 16 MiB per file, 500,000 triangles, 2,000 nodes, 32 models / 128 MiB per library. Animations, skins, cameras, lights, Draco/Meshopt compression, KTX2 and instancing are unsupported. Use dimensions between 1 cm and 60 m wide/deep, up to 20 m high. Models too large for the room remain in the library but cannot be placed; grow the room first. Library-definition removal is not implemented.
- Undo/redo with the toolbar or Cmd/Ctrl+Z and Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+Z. History lasts for the current page session. Reset restores both the original dimensions and furniture and can also be undone.
- Warehouse dimensions and furniture positions persist in this browser's local storage. Older furniture-only saves remain supported. Export downloads a version-3 JSON record, including the current room dimensions and the original GLB bytes (base64) for every uploaded model placed in that layout. Unplaced library models are not included; keep their source files. JSON import is not implemented. If model storage cannot be read or a referenced model is missing, layout saving pauses to preserve the existing save; reload after restoring browser storage.
- Objects stay within the estimated room boundary. Amber outlines warn about overlapping furniture; they do not certify circulation, fire egress, accessibility or capacity. Up to 200 furniture objects are supported.

The source photo and its people are not included or uploaded. Built-in geometry and textures are generated locally; imported GLBs are read locally without external resource requests. There is no backend, room scanning, free-text planning, or account integration in this first prototype. The UI switches camera perspectives; it does not implement first-person walking controls.

## Verification

`corepack pnpm test` tests rotated boundaries, collision detection, initial furniture placement, saved-layout validation, activity capacity, generated seating counts, non-overlapping arrangements, GLB container checks, external-resource rejection, invalid node hierarchies and custom model footprints. Crowd tests cover counts, seating overflow, placement shortfalls and moving-person clearance against rotated and imported furniture. Tour tests cover furniture clearance, camera continuity, room sizes and blocked entrances. Browser QA also covers playback, pause, replay, scrubbing and manual exploration. Browser QA covers the three views, adding, dragging, rotating, editing coordinates, local persistence and history controls. GLB browser QA also covers importing the generated Chesterfield, actual dimensions, rotation/duplication/undo, IndexedDB restoration, invalid-file feedback, embedded textures, byte-preserving export and preserving a saved layout when its library is missing.

Static deployment uses `dist/`. The existing private prototype is at https://brickworks-warehouse-planner.lacypixie.chatgpt.site (ChatGPT sign-in required). This repository is independent of the Octo Devlabs monorepo; the original source is preserved there.

## Activity layouts

Choose **Hackathon** or **Theatre night**, enter the number of people, then select **Apply activity layout**. The same venue is rearranged and shown overhead; all generated furniture remains editable in 3D. Applying a template replaces furniture and can be undone. Activity details persist with the layout and are included in JSON export; manually editing furniture labels the result as custom.

- Hackathon: five table groups with five joined worktables each, six chairs per table, seat 150 people (30 per group). In rooms at least 14 × 28 m, the layout roughly follows the reference photograph: two long table banks down each side, a fifth across the foreground, two four-couch conversation areas in the middle and coffee/tea/snack tables along the right wall. Narrower rooms keep the compact five-row arrangement with two lounges at the far end. The eight couches add 24 lounge seats. This grouped arrangement fits rooms at least 11 × 22 m, including the default 12 × 22 m venue; smaller rooms use a compact arrangement and report any seating shortfall. Rooms below 11 m wide or 18 m long omit couches and refreshments. The eight built-in couch models have different shapes and colours: oxblood Chesterfield, sage Scandinavian, mustard channel, navy tuxedo, cream cloud, terracotta mid-century, lavender modular and teal timber. Add them individually from **Lounge & refreshments**; all support editing, undo, browser saving and JSON export. Desk seats are filled before lounge seats in the crowd preview. Existing saved furniture is preserved; select **Hackathon → 150 → Apply activity layout** to use the revised template.
- **Compare relaxed layouts:** under Hackathon, select **Compare relaxed layouts** to see three floor-plan previews side by side, then **Try** a style to apply it in the editable 3D room. **Creative pockets** mixes six end-to-end table pairs (12 tables) with 13 singles at 150 attendees, gentle angles and sofa corners; **Warehouse commons** mixes table directions and social lounge pockets; **Loose & lively** staggers and angles its tables. Each style keeps 25 tables, 150 desk chairs, eight couches and three refreshment tables at full attendance. Relaxed styles need at least 14 × 28 m; smaller rooms show the requirement rather than shrinking furniture. The original arrangement remains under **Photo reference**. Choices persist with the layout, export in event metadata and support undo/redo. The previews use the exact generated furniture footprints; no model or image requests are made.
- Theatre night: a 6 × 2.5 m stage and chair rows facing it, 180 seats at the default 12 × 22 m dimensions.
- Templates adapt to the current room dimensions and the 200-object editor limit. Requests above the resulting limits show the available seating and shortfall rather than packing in overlapping objects. These limits describe the templates, not the venue's maximum occupancy. Circulation gaps are illustrative; the estimated room, door access, accessibility and permitted event capacity must be checked on site.

A later version can interpret free text (for example, “a wedding for 80 people”) into an activity, guest count and furniture requirements, then validate and render the arrangement through the same geometry rules. Free-text planning and wedding furniture are not implemented yet. No AI service or API key is required for the activity selector.
